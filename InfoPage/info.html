The basics

What does BMI mean?

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a number calculated from a person's weight and height. It is commonly used as a screening measure to group weight ranges for adults.

The formula

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²

Height is measured in metres and squared.

Putting it in context

Why is BMI important?

BMI can offer a quick, consistent way to start a conversation about weight-related health risks.

A healthcare professional may use BMI alongside other information to decide whether more assessment or support could be helpful. It is a screening tool, not a diagnosis.

Adult BMI ranges

BMI categories

These commonly used ranges are for adults. They may not apply to everyone.

BMI range Category What it may indicate
Below 18.5 Underweight Below the usual reference range
18.5–24.9 Normal weight Within the usual reference range
25.0–29.9 Overweight Above the usual reference range
30.0 and above Obesity Well above the usual reference range

BMI ranges are a general guide. Individual health depends on more than a single number.

Everyday wellbeing

Small habits, lasting benefits

General ideas to support wellbeing. Choose what is realistic and suitable for you.

01

Build balanced meals

Include a variety of vegetables and fruit, whole grains, and protein-rich foods. Enjoy a range of foods that suit your needs and preferences.

02

Move in ways you enjoy

Walking, dancing, cycling, or other activities can all count. Start at a comfortable pace and build movement into your routine when you can.

03

Stay hydrated

Drink fluids regularly through the day. Your needs can vary with activity, weather, and health, so follow any advice from your care team.

04

Make room for sleep

A regular, relaxing bedtime routine can support good sleep. Try to keep a consistent schedule that works for your life.

i

A note about BMI

BMI is a screening measure and not a complete assessment of an individual's health. It does not directly measure body fat or account for every factor, such as age, muscle mass, or individual background. Talk with a qualified healthcare professional for personal guidance.
